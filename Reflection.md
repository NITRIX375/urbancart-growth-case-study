# Reflection: why I still want this role

Rigor is not the price I pay to do this work; it is the part I like. The moment in this case study I enjoyed most was finding that 35 accounts on the company's own email domain were quietly making the worst channel look like the second best. Nobody told me to look there. The numbers were "right" before I did, and they would have sent ₹1.7 lakh a quarter to the wrong place. Catching that is the job, and it only happens if you check the data before you trust it, every single time.

I want to work on growth and marketing data specifically because the feedback loop is short and honest. A recommendation gets acted on next quarter, the spend moves, and the numbers tell you whether you were right.

I also want the engineering half of it. Doing this analysis once took an afternoon; doing it every week without me is the real product, and the gap between a notebook and a pipeline a CMO can trust unsupervised is where I want to put my effort. Building an AI tool that answers her questions but cannot invent a number is exactly the kind of constrained, careful problem I find satisfying.

Hard work does not put me off when the work compounds. A clean metrics layer, tests that catch the next bad export, and a leader who no longer waits on the data team: that compounds. I want to build it.
